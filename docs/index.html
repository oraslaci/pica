Got Questions?

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about Pica, privacy, and how it handles your photos.

Is Pica really like Google Picasa?

Yes! Pica was born out of love for the original Google Picasa 3.9 after Google retired it in 2016.

It recreates the things people missed most: instant startup, smooth 120 FPS scrolling through tens of thousands of photos, offline folder-based organization, 1-click quick fixes ("I'm Feeling Lucky", Red-Eye, Straighten), and automatic face grouping — modernized with a clean darkroom design.

Do I have to upload my photos to the cloud?

No. Never. Pica is strictly 100% offline. It has zero cloud storage, zero tracking, and zero telemetry. You do not need to create an account, log in, or give your email address. Every single photo stays on your computer hard drive.

Will Pica move, delete, or rename my existing photo folders?

Never. Unlike some modern apps that import your photos into a proprietary library or change your folder names, Pica scans your existing folders in place. Your files stay exactly where you put them on your PC or external drives.

How much does Pica cost? Are there hidden fees?

Pica is 100% free for personal use. There are no monthly subscriptions, no locked features, and no banner ads.

Can I undo my edits if I don't like how a photo turned out?

Yes, absolutely. All edits in Pica are non-destructive. Whenever you crop, straighten, or enhance a photo, Pica automatically saves an untouched copy of your original file into a hidden .pica_originals safety folder. You can revert back to your original photo anytime, even months later.

How does face recognition work without an internet connection?

Pica includes small, fast artificial intelligence models built directly inside the application. When you open a folder, your computer's own processor detects and groups similar faces locally in seconds. No photos or face embeddings ever leave your device.

What operating systems are supported?

Pica is cross-platform and runs natively on:

  • Windows: Windows 10 and Windows 11 (64-bit and ARM64)
  • macOS: Apple Silicon (M1/M2/M3/M4) and Intel Macs (macOS 11+)
  • Linux: Ubuntu, Debian, Fedora, Arch, and modern distributions via AppImage, .deb, and .rpm
